## 2. What this is

Authors say (`0_main.tex` L107, L116–122, L167–170; `1_intro.tex` L17; `3_methodology.tex` L16): SpecMD ("Speculative MoE Decoding", titled *SpecMD: A Comprehensive Study On Speculative Expert Prefetching*, Duc Hoang, Ajay Jaiswal, Mohammad Samragh, Minsik Cho, Apple, arXiv:2602.03921v1, 3 Feb 2026) is a PyTorch gate-hook benchmarking framework for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) expert caching across four policy dimensions: routing, prefetching, eviction, and cache-miss handling. Authors say (`0_main.tex` L169; `3_methodology.tex` L123–129): "MoE expert access is not consistent with temporal locality assumptions (e.g LRU, LFU)" because each forward pass traverses layers sequentially ($0 \to 1 \to \dots \to L-1$), causing temporal or purely spatial policies to evict soon-needed experts in the same forward pass ("collision misses"). Authors say (`3_methodology.tex` L125–149): their proposed **Least-Stale (LS)** eviction policy splits cached experts into a *stale queue* (previous forward passes) and a *current queue* (ongoing pass or prefetched), evicting stale experts first in layer-position FIFO order. Authors say (`3_methodology.tex` L16): "open-sourcing SpecMD is underway."

Code availability (`3_methodology.tex` L16): footnote "open-sourcing SpecMD is underway."
  - **Algorithm 1 (`3_methodology.tex` L76–102, *Gate Hook Processing*):** Input: Gate outputs `(experts, weights, logits, router_weights)` at layer $l$. Phase 1 (Routing modification): `logits' <- RoutingPolicy.apply(l, logits)`; `experts' <- top_k(softmax(logits'))`; `weights' <- router_weights[experts']` (dual-logit semantics, `6_appendix.tex` L12: modified logits select experts, original router weights scale outputs). Phase 2 (Cache management): for each $e \in \text{experts}'$, if $e \notin \text{GPU\_Cache}$, call `MissPolicy.handle(e)`. Phase 3 (Prefetch submission): for each target layer $t \in \mathcal{T}$ (in experiments $\mathcal{T} = \{l+1\}$, `3_methodology.tex` L61), `predicted <- PrefetchPolicy.predict(t)` and `PrefetchQueue.submit(predicted, t)`.
  - **Collision miss definition (`3_methodology.tex` L129):** "a type of cache miss where experts are evicted and then immediately needed again in the same forward pass." Specifically, during a single forward pass across layers $0 \to 15$, demand loads or prefetches in layer $l$ evict cached experts (or prefetched experts for upcoming layers $>l$) that are subsequently requested before the forward pass completes (`3_methodology.tex` L123–129, `4_evaluation.tex` L32–35).
  - **Eviction policies compared (`3_methodology.tex` L50–57, L125–149; `6_appendix.tex` L26–40):** Five baselines in Appendix B (`6_appendix.tex` L29–40): **LRU** (Least Recently Used; typo "Least Freqently Used" in `3_methodology.tex` L54), **LFU** (Least Frequently Used over inference history), **LHU** (Least High-precision Used, from HOBBIT), **FLD** (Farthest Layer Distance, evicts experts with largest $|l_{cached} - l_{current}|$), and **SB** (Score-Based, evicts lowest historical gate score). **FIFO** is *not* a separate baseline in the paper; instead, FIFO by layer-expert position is the internal ordering inside each of Least-Stale's two priority heaps (`3_methodology.tex` L136–149: *stale queue* for previous forward passes evicted first, *current queue* for ongoing pass / prefetched experts evicted only when stale queue is empty).
  - **Prefetch rules (`3_methodology.tex` L59–71, `6_appendix.tex` L20):** Apply layer $l+1$'s router gate to layer $l$'s activations to obtain predicted router scores for layer $l+1$. Two strategies: (1) **Top-$k$ with overfetch factor $m$**: prefetch top $\text{round}(m \cdot k)$ experts by predicted weight ($k=8$ for OLMoE; $m=1.5 \implies 12$ experts, $m=2.0 \implies 16$ experts, `3_methodology.tex` L68, `4_evaluation.tex` L71); (2) **Score-based**: prefetch all experts in layer $l+1$ whose predicted softmax score exceeds a percentile threshold (default 80th percentile, `3_methodology.tex` L70).
  - **Cache-aware routing equation (`3_methodology.tex` L34–38, Eq. 1):** $z' = z + \lambda \cdot \Delta_{\text{avg}} \cdot \mathbbm{1}_C$, where $z = G(x)$ is original router logits, $\mathbbm{1}_C \in \{0,1\}^{|E|}$ indicates cached experts, $\lambda \in [0.1, 0.5]$ (`4_evaluation.tex` L142), and $\Delta_{\text{avg}}$ is the historical average logit value.
  - **Evaluation setup (`0_main.tex` L154, L169; `4_evaluation.tex` L10–16, L86–104; `6_appendix.tex` L107–129):** Four models including `OLMoE-1B-7B` (`\olmoe` macro in `0_main.tex` L154 is `OLMoE-1B-7B-0125-Instruct`; Table 2 specs: 16 layers, 64 experts/layer, top-$k=8$, 12 MB/expert at 16-bit, 12.0 GB expert params, 0.89 GB non-expert params). Evaluated on 100 examples each of GSM8K, TruthfulQA, and NaturalQuestions on an A100 80GB GPU with software-limited cache capacities of **1%, 5% (0.6 GB for OLMoE), and 25%** of total experts and **5 GB/s** transfer bandwidth. Published collision rates at 5% capacity (`4_evaluation.tex` L22): LS 1.6–1.9%, LRU 4.5–12.6%, SB 42.6–60.9%.
- **What it showed:** Prediction hit on all items except two nuances revealed by reading the full TeX: (1) FIFO is not an independent baseline policy in the paper, but the intra-queue layer-position ordering inside Least-Stale (`3_methodology.tex` L147), whereas the paper's five baselines are LRU, LFU, LHU, FLD, and SB (`6_appendix.tex` L29–40); (2) a collision miss specifically means an expert evicted and then needed again within the *same forward pass* (`3_methodology.tex` L129), or a prefetched expert evicted before its target layer executes in that pass (`4_evaluation.tex` L32).

- **Against the prediction:** held: prompt and decode agree to `0.03` point (predicted `0.5`); score filter averages `12.75-13.06` experts globally and `13.03` per token; overfetch `1.5x` precision is two thirds of recall; at 5% prefetch lifts `LS`/`LRU` from `0%`; `LFU`/`SB` suffer `>25%` collisions; overfetch wastes `>1.8x` more loads. **Missed:** top-8 recall `84%` (predicted `35-60%`); Variant B gain `+0.13` point (predicted `+3-10`); overfetch recall gain `+9.6` points (predicted `+10-15`); hit rates after prefetch were higher than predicted (`79%` at 5% vs `40-65%`; `87.65%` at 25% vs `70-80%`); and at 1% `LS` lost to `LRU` by `69` points, where I had expected `LS` to have fewer collisions than `LRU`.
- **What it showed:** Adjacent-layer hidden states are similar enough (cosine `0.67` rising to `0.87`) that the next layer's own gate applied to the current activations names most of the next layer's experts (`84%` of the top-8, `94%` with 12 fetched); one extra normalisation step changes that by a tenth of a point. With that prediction, a cache of 5% of the experts goes from `0%` to `79%` hits in a token-by-token stream because the prefetched 8 experts are the ones requested next. The ranking of policies in these runs depends on the rule set: `LS`, `LRU` and `FLD` make the same choices at $C=51$ (identical counts), and the `LS` vs `LRU` gap at $C=10$ (`9.75%` vs `78.89%`) comes from how the study's `LS` rules (the ones I re-implemented as `LS2` in section 10) treat prefetched (current) entries; it is not a statement about the paper's `LS`. Simulated hit counts only: no latency, bandwidth or compute overlap is modelled.

### E8: The baseline repository's eviction groups, read from source and replayed on the recorded traces

- **Question:** The paper cites Eliseev et al. 2023 (`dvmazur/mixtral-offloading`) as its LRU baseline and describes it as "keeping a fixed number of recently-used experts across all layers" (`tex_part1.txt` line 245). What does that repository's cache code do, and how does that scheme perform on the OLMoE traces?
- **Prediction** (in `work/predictions.txt`, written before the run): decode stream with `m=16` slots per layer ($C=256$) `54% +/- 1` (the study's hits-first per-layer LRU gave `54.26%`); `m=3` about `11% +/- 2`; `m=4` about `18% +/- 3`; the choice of which experts start on the GPU changes the rate by under `0.1` point; processing a call's experts sorted by index instead of rank order changes it by under `0.5` point at `m=3`; prompt stream at `m=16` at least `96%`.
- **Read** (clone at `/root/dm-study/specmd/repo/mixtral-offloading`, commit `ce54518`, has a LICENSE; `src/expert_cache.py` 201 lines, `src/build_model.py` 241 lines, read by me):
  - `build_model.py:233` registers every expert with `eviction_group=layer_idx`; `expert_cache.py` keeps one `EvictionGroupInfo` per group with `main_infos` (on-GPU experts, an `OrderedDict` from least to most recently used) and `offloaded_infos`.
  - Experts with index `< offload_per_layer` start offloaded; the rest start on the GPU, in ascending order (`do_offload = expert_idx < offload_per_layer`). The notebook sets `main_size = num_layers * (num_experts - offload_per_layer)` with `offload_per_layer = 4` (Mixtral: 8 experts, 4 stay on the GPU per layer).
  - `load_experts(*uids)` asserts that all uids belong to one group; it calls `mark_used` for every uid first (a uid in `main_infos` is a hit and moves to the MRU end, otherwise a miss), then swaps each missing expert in, evicting `choose_expert_to_evict()` = the first entry of that group's `main_infos`. `_swap` asserts the two experts share a group, so GPU slots never move between layers.
  - So in the code the cache is **a set of independent per-layer LRU pools of equal size**, not one LRU shared across layers. The paper's sentence ("a fixed number of recently-used experts across all layers") can be read either way; the code is the per-layer reading, and E6's global-pool LRU is the other reading.
- **Run:** [e8_baseline_group_lru.py](../../../research/hardware-data-movement/specmd/work/e8_baseline_group_lru.py) (a reimplementation of exactly the rules above, nothing else) -> [e8_baseline_group_lru.out](../../../research/hardware-data-movement/specmd/results/e8_baseline_group_lru.out).
- **Observation** (hit rate %, rank order / high-index start; `m` slots per layer, total `C = 16 m`):
  - decode stream: `m=1` `3.37`, `m=2` `7.28`, `m=3` `11.56`, `m=4` `16.36`, `m=8` `39.14`, `m=16` `54.28`, `m=32` `76.50`.
  - prompt stream: `m=1` `3.37`, `m=2` `7.29`, `m=3` `11.56`, `m=4` `16.36`, `m=8` `39.12`, `m=16` `54.29`, `m=32` `76.51`.
  - Starting with low instead of high expert indices on the GPU: identical to two decimals. Processing a call's experts in sorted order instead of rank order: `m=1` `4.73`, `m=2` `9.32`, `m=3` `13.91`, `m=4` `18.51` (decode) versus `3.37 / 7.28 / 11.56 / 16.36` in rank order, i.e. `+1.4` to `+2.4` points while $m<8$ (the experts per call), and `+0.0` to `+0.1` for $m\ge8$.

- **What it showed:** (a) per-layer pools are independent, so each group sees the same sequence of calls whether the global order is token-major or layer-major; decode and prompt streams therefore give the same hit rate (`54.28%` vs `54.29%` at `m=16`), whereas a global LRU of the same total size gets `51.04%` (decode) and `96.98%` (prompt) at $C=256$ (E6): partitioning removes the dependence on traversal order but caps each layer at `m` slots, and during a layer-major prompt sweep a global cache can give the whole capacity to the layer in progress. (b) The within-call order effect is the baseline's own `mark_used`-then-swap rule: when fewer slots than experts per call exist, which experts are marked first decides which are evicted by the swaps. (c) The paper's one-sentence description of this baseline is ambiguous between the two readings; the code settles it.

## 4. How it works

(From my own reading of the paper's methodology section (`tex_part1.txt` lines 290-420), the OLMoE router code, the baseline repository, and from E1-E8. Numbers cite the experiment.)

**The setting.** OLMoE-1B-7B-0924 has 16 layers with 64 experts each (`1,024` experts); a token activates the top 8 of 64 in every layer (`128` experts per token), each `12.00 MiB` in bf16 (E2). The router is one linear layer from the hidden state to 64 logits, a softmax, and `topk(8)`; the selected experts' probabilities are not renormalised (`norm_topk_prob=False`) (E2, E3). A cache of `C` experts is asked, per token, for those 128 experts in layer order `0 -> 15` (E4).

**What the access stream looks like** (E4). Consecutive tokens share `39.20%` of their 8 experts in the same layer (shuffling the tokens leaves `20.80%`; independent uniform choice would give `12.50%`), so locality inside a layer is real. Seen by a cache shared across layers in token order, every reuse is separated by at least the `15 x 8 = 120` experts of the other layers, so the minimum stack distance is `120` (measured `min = 120`) and a recency-based global cache of $C\le120$ gets no hit. In layer-major order (prompt processing) the same reuses are close: `27%` of requests have stack distance below 8.

**The policies** (paper section 3, E5, E6). `LRU`, `FIFO`, `LFU` (frequency over the history), `FLD` (farthest layer from the current one), `SB` (lowest historical gate score), `Belady` (evict the item used farthest in the future, an upper bound), and `Least-Stale` (`LS`): cached experts used in an earlier forward pass are "stale", the others "current"; stale ones go first, and within a queue the order follows layer position. The paper does not say how stale entries from different earlier passes are ordered or whether the current queue is LRU or FIFO by layer. Two readings of those details give hit rates that differ by `7.0` points at $C=256$ decode (`46.82%` vs `53.86%`) and by `2.4` points at $C=51$ prompt (`92.88%` vs `90.46%`) (section 10).

**Collision miss** (paper, E5): an expert that was evicted and then needed again in the same forward pass. The simulator counts two operational variants separately (resident at pass start and evicted before use; inserted earlier in the pass and evicted before use); the paper gives no formula.

**Speculative prefetch** (paper, E7): apply layer `l+1`'s router to layer `l`'s hidden state and fetch the top-`k` (or `1.5 k`, or all above a score percentile). Measured (E7): the prediction's top-8 hits `84.4%` of the real top-8 of the next layer (`60%` for `0 -> 1`, about `90%` from layer 7 on).

**The baseline** (E8, read): one LRU pool per layer, equal size, each holding a fixed number of that layer's experts; hits move an expert to the MRU end; misses swap in and evict the group's LRU.

## 5. What surprised me and what I got wrong
- **Memory (E2):** the model's resident memory was `25.09 GiB`, not the `15-18 GB` I (first wave) expected.
- **Library change (E2):** `transformers 5.20.2` stores the 64 experts of a layer as fused 3-D tensors (`OlmoeExperts`), so per-expert indexing failed until the script was changed.
- **Locality stronger than predicted (E4):** lag-1 overlap `39.2%` (predicted `20-35%`), Gini `0.3845` (predicted `0.15-0.30`).
- **Two arithmetic errors in my predictions (E5):** Zipf top-16 mass is `80.08%`, not `65.43%`; Belady on a cyclic sweep of $C+1$ items has steady-state $(C-1)/C=93.75\%$, not $(C-1)/(C+1)$.
- **A first `LS` implementation ran fine and encoded the wrong rule (E5):** it gave `41.74%` at $C=256$ until the stale ordering was changed (`56.05%` on the E5 trace slice). A script that runs is not a check on a rule.
- **E6 predictions that missed:** `LS` in prompt-style at 5% (`90.46%`, predicted `92-93%`); `LFU` and `SB` in prompt-style at 5% (`18.14%` and `11.11%`, predicted `40-60%`). The earlier summary line said "predictions hit"; corrected in E6.
- **E7 prediction accuracy (E7a):** I (first wave) predicted `35-60%`; measured `84.4%`. Applying the next layer's RMSNorm to the residual (Variant B) instead of the direct gate input (Variant A) changed top-8 recall by `+0.13` point (predicted `+3-10`).
- **E8:** order of experts within a call matters by up to `2.4` points when slots per layer are below 8; the prompt stream at 16 slots per layer gave `54.29%`, not `>= 96%`.
- **The "decode-style" stream is teacher-forced** (the recorded text is fed one token at a time with a KV cache); it is not the model's own generation.
