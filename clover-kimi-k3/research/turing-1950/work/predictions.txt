## E4: Section 7 — Child Machine, Reward/Punishment Information Bound vs Unemotional Symbolic Channel, Imperative Inference, and Random vs Systematic Search
Timestamp (before run): 2026-10-26 14:48 UTC
Predictions:
1. Reward/Punishment alone vs Unemotional Symbolic Channel (Section 7, p. 20):
   - Teaching a child machine a target mapping of $K$ binary actions/responses (or an $L$-symbol sequence like a line of "Casabianca" over an alphabet of size $V$) using ONLY binary reward/punishment signals requires $\Omega(L \cdot V)$ or at least $H$ bits of binary feedback signals (information-theoretic lower bound: each binary reward/punishment conveys at most 1 bit), whereas transmitting instructions over an "unemotional" symbolic channel once the machine has learned a small command vocabulary ($V_0$ symbols) reduces total reward/punishment signals by $>10\times$ as message length $L$ grows.
2. Built-in Logical Inference with Epistemic Statuses & Imperatives (Section 7, pp. 20–21):
   - A child machine storing propositions with Turing's five statuses (`well_established`, `conjecture`, `proved_theorem`, `given_by_authority`, `no_belief_value`) plus `imperative` rules ("Do your homework now" + "Teacher says 'Do your homework now'" + "Everything that teacher says is true") will automatically promote "Do your homework now" to `well_established` and execute the action, and applying Turing's meta-imperative ("If one method has been proved to be quicker than another, do not use the slower method") will prune proof search steps on syllogism problems ("When Socrates is mentioned, use the syllogism in Barbara").
3. Random vs Systematic Search in [50, 200] (Section 7, p. 22):
   - For Turing's concrete example on p. 22 ("find a number between 50 and 200 which was equal to the square of the sum of its digits"), the unique solution in `[50, 200]` is **`81`** (`(8+1)^2 = 81`). Systematic search starting at 51 takes `81 - 51 + 1 = 31` evaluations (`150` if scanning downward from 200 across the "enormous block without any solutions" `82..200`). When there are $k$ solutions clustered after a dead block of size $B$, memoryless random search requires expected $N / k$ trials regardless of block order, beating systematic search whenever $B > N / k$.
