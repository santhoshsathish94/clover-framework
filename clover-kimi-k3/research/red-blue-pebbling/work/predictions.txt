[2026-10-26 06:15 UTC] E2 (Hand-made move sequences on repo engine vs minimal paper engine):
- Question: How do the repository's PebblingGame engine and a minimal engine implementing Hong & Kung's exact Section 2 rules respond to hand-made legal and illegal move sequences probing each rule (R1 input, R2 output, R3 compute, R4 delete, red limit S, compute without all predecessors red, recomputation, ending condition)?
- Prediction:
  1. Both engines will reject computing a node when a predecessor is completely unpebbled, both will reject exceeding red/register capacity S, and both will accept deleting a pebble and recomputing the node later.
  2. Six differences will appear when running the test sequences:
     (a) Initial state & R1 cost: Paper starts with B=inputs, R=empty, and R1 costs 1 I/O; repo starts all None, PLACE(source, register) costs 1 pJ, and TRANSFER(v, register) from sram/hbm costs 0 pJ.
     (b) Predecessor color for R3: Paper rejects R3(v) when a predecessor has only a blue pebble (not red); repo accepts PLACE(v, register) when predecessors are in sram or hbm (not register).
     (c) Direct compute into slow memory: Paper R3 only places red pebbles (cost 0); repo PLACE(v, sram/hbm) computes directly into sram (5 pJ) or hbm (100 pJ) even when register_slots=0.
     (d) Simultaneous red+blue vs move on R2: Paper R2(v) adds v to B while keeping v in R (red count unchanged); repo TRANSFER(v, sram/hbm) moves v out of register (decrementing register usage).
     (e) Deleting an input's blue pebble: In the paper engine, R4(blue, source) removes the input from B so a subsequent R1(source) is rejected; in the repo, REMOVE(source) followed by PLACE(source, register) is accepted.
     (f) Ending condition: Paper is complete only when R == empty and B == outputs; repo sets done=True as soon as all sinks have pebbles in any tier (including register) and while non-sinks still hold pebbles.

     - Fitted exponents on blocked (b=floor(sqrt(S/3))): alpha ~ 3.00 in n, beta ~ -0.50 in S, with constant IO / (n^3 / sqrt(S)) ~ 3.46 under OPT and ~ 4.0..4.8 under LRU (tile ijk).
  3. LRU vs OPT mechanism: when 3*b^2 == S under intra-tile order ijk, B from tile kk-1 is touched during i=b-1 after rows 0..b-2 of C, so LRU evicts live C rows at the start of tile kk; switching intra-tile order to kij (which touches all of C during the last k iteration) will eliminate most of the LRU penalty over OPT.
